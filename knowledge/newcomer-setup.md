# Hao Knowledge Base: Newcomer Setup in China
2026-10-22 · Brown

## How to use this guide
This is Hao's knowledge base for helping newcomers settle in China; the agent should guide users through the steps below in this order, because each step unlocks the next. Rules change often, so the agent should treat figures here as a starting point, check the user's city and university, and point to official sources for anything legal.
**Recommended order for a new student**
1. Before the flight: visa, Alipay and WeChat Pay linked to a foreign card, apps installed.
2. Day 1: accommodation registration with the police or dorm, within 24 hours.
3. First week: a Chinese SIM card (+86 number) with real-name registration.
4. First week: health check and residence permit application (X1 visa holders, within 30 days of entry).
5. First two weeks: Chinese bank account, then link it to Alipay and WeChat Pay.
6. Ongoing: register again after moving or renewing documents; extend the permit at least 30 days before expiry.
Tourists only need step 1, plus a travel eSIM or tourist SIM; hotels handle their registration.

## Before arrival
The most useful setup happens before the flight: the right visa, Alipay and WeChat Pay linked, and key apps installed. Students must come on a study visa; visa-free entry does not allow study ([CNTO, August 2026](https://www.cnto.org.uk/last-news-en/china-expands-240-hour-transit-and-hainan-30-day-visa-free-entry-policies/)).
**Visa basics**

| Type | Who | Key rule |
|---|---|---|
| X1 study visa | Students staying over 180 days | Valid 30 days after entry; convert to a residence permit (see Residence section) |
| X2 study visa | Students staying 180 days or less | Stay on the visa; no residence permit |
| 30-day visa-free | Tourists from eligible countries | Tourism, business, family visits; list runs to 31 December 2026 |
| 240-hour visa-free transit | 57 countries, via 65 ports | Must have an onward ticket to a third country; stay in permitted areas |
| Hainan 30-day visa-free | 61 countries | Only within Hainan province |

Country lists change often, so the agent should check the user's nationality against the current National Immigration Administration list rather than a stored answer.
**Checklist before flying**
- Passport valid at least 6 more months, plus photocopies and passport photos
- Admission letter and JW201/JW202 form (students: originals are needed for the residence permit)
- Install WeChat and Alipay, link a Visa or Mastercard, and finish passport verification
- Tell your bank you will use the card in China
- Install Amap, DiDi, Trip.com and a translation app (Android: before arriving)
- Keep the home SIM active with roaming for SMS codes
- Carry some cash in CNY for the first day
- Save the university international office contact and dorm address in Chinese
**Agent tip:** students should not arrive many days before check-in, because the 30-day X1 clock starts at entry.

## Alipay
Foreigners can use Alipay with a foreign phone number and a foreign Visa or Mastercard, with no Chinese bank account needed. Payments of 200 CNY or less are free; above that, a 3% fee applies to the whole amount ([Tianwen, tested July 2026](https://tianwenwangluo.com/en/alipay-for-foreigners/)).
**What the user needs:** the standard Alipay app (not Alipay HK), a home phone number that can receive SMS abroad, a passport, and an international credit or debit card.
**Setup steps**
1. Download Alipay from the App Store or Google Play and register with the home number and SMS code. The app switches to English for foreign numbers.
2. Add an international card: number, expiry, and name exactly as printed on the card. A small temporary charge may appear and is reversed.
3. Verify identity: Me → Settings → Account and Security → Identity Information. Upload the passport photo page and complete the face check. This unlocks the full limits. Menu labels change between app versions.
4. Pay: tap Scan to read the merchant's QR code, or show your own payment code for the cashier to scan.
5. Optional: activate the city's metro or bus card inside Alipay for public transport.
**Limits and fees**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Fee-free per payment | Up to 200 CNY |
| Fee above 200 CNY | 3% of the whole amount (plus the user's own bank's foreign fee) |
| Single payment limit | 5,000 USD |
| Yearly limit | 50,000 USD |

The limits were raised in March 2024 from 1,000 / 10,000 USD after a central bank directive ([China Daily](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202403/02/WS65e26803a31082fc043ba22f.html)).
**Cards that work:** Visa, Mastercard, Discover, Diners Club, JCB and UnionPay International (UnionPay is exempt from the 3% fee). American Express is reported to work but is not on every official page, so users should bring a Visa or Mastercard as backup.
**Agent tips**
- Tell users to set up Alipay at home, days before flying, so they can call their bank if binding is blocked.
- Skip the old TourCard prepaid wallet: about 5% top-up fee and a 10,000 CNY balance cap. Direct card binding is better.
- Foreign cards cannot send money to friends or red packets; some merchant types also refuse them.
- Never borrow someone else's account or buy a verified one. Accounts get frozen.

## WeChat and WeChat Pay
WeChat Pay accepts foreign cards since July 2023, with the same fee rule as Alipay: free up to 200 CNY, 3% of the whole amount above that ([Tianwen, July 2026](https://tianwenwangluo.com/en/wechat-pay-for-foreigners/)). WeChat is also the main messaging app, so newcomers need it for classmates, landlords and school groups.
**Setup steps** (path from the [WeChat Help Center](https://help.wechat.com/cgi-bin/micromsg-bin/oshelpcenter?opcode=2&id=2307252mur7f2307257zfyfe&lang=en&plat=ios&Channel=helpcenter))
1. Download WeChat and register with any phone number that receives SMS; a +86 number is not required. New foreign accounts are sometimes asked to be verified by an existing WeChat user scanning a QR code, so a classmate or friend can help.
2. Go to Me → Services (or Pay and Services) → Wallet → Bank Cards → Add a Bank Card.
3. Agree to the terms, enter identity details, then the card number and billing details.
4. Verify identity with a passport or Foreign Permanent Resident ID card. Passport users upload a photo for review, which takes minutes to 72 hours.
5. Set a 6-digit payment PIN, then pay by scanning merchant QR codes or showing your payment code.
If the Wallet entry is missing: Me → Settings → General → Manage Services → turn on WeChat Pay.
**Limits and fees**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Fee-free per payment | Up to 200 CNY |
| Fee above 200 CNY | 3% of the whole amount; refunds return the fee proportionally |
| Single payment limit (WeChat help page) | 6,500 CNY |
| Monthly limit | 50,000 CNY |
| Yearly limit | 65,000 CNY |
| New-user offer (Beijing measure, March 2026) | 3% fee waived for 60 days, daily up to 1,000 CNY |

The central bank ceiling is 5,000 USD per payment, but WeChat's own help page still lists the CNY figures above, so the agent should plan with those.
**What works on a foreign card**

| Feature | Status |
|---|---|
| Scan merchant QR / show payment code | Works |
| In-store and in-app purchases | Works |
| Red packets (hongbao) | Not supported |
| Transfers to friends | Not supported |
| Mini-programs (Didi, Meituan, trains) | Varies by merchant |

**Cards accepted** ([Beijing government guide](https://english.beijing.gov.cn/travellinginbeijing/essentials/inboundtourismfacilitationmeasures/202603/t20260326_4566766.html)): Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover, JCB, Diners Club and international UnionPay. Virtual and prepaid cards are often rejected; the name must match the passport.
**Agent tip:** recommend Alipay as the main wallet and WeChat Pay as backup. Red packets and transfers need a Chinese bank account, which is a reason students should open one (see Bank account).

## Sending money from home to China
Newcomers can move money from home into Alipay, WeChat Pay or a Chinese bank card with remittance apps; for Tanzanians, Selcom Pesa added transfers to China via Alipay and WeChat Pay in its August 2026 update ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/selcom-pesa/id6737184776)). The big catch: many services only pay Alipay or WeChat accounts verified with a Chinese Resident ID, so a foreigner's own foreign-verified wallet may not receive ([Wise](https://wise.com/cn/send-money/send-money-to-tanzania), [Taptap Send](https://help.taptapsend.com/en/receiving-countries/how-can-i-send-money-to-china)). Selcom accepts a passport for the recipient, so a foreign student may be able to receive into their own wallet; the agent should still confirm the recipient rule in each app before advising this.
**Tanzania: Selcom Pesa**
1. Open a Selcom Pesa account in the app with a NIDA card or Tanzanian passport, plus fingerprint and face check ([Selcom Pesa](https://www.selcompesa.app/selcom-pesa-new-landing-page)).
2. Add money: from M-Pesa or other mobile money (Send Money to Bank → Selcom Microfinance Bank), from a bank, or with cash at a Selcom Huduma agent or Selcom Xperience Center.
3. In the app, choose Send Internationally → China, then Alipay or WeChat Pay. Selcom asks for one of two things: an invoice (when paying a supplier), or the recipient's passport and their Alipay or WeChat ID. Have these ready before starting.
4. Check the fee, rate and recipient requirements shown in the app before confirming; Selcom's site does not yet publish China fees (its India transfer is a flat 5.99 USD).
Help: toll-free 0800 714 888, WhatsApp +255 699 077 988.
**Options by country**

| From | Service | Pays into | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tanzania | Selcom Pesa | Alipay, WeChat Pay | Cash top-up at Selcom agents; China added Aug 2026 |
| Tanzania | M-Pesa Global Payment (Vodacom) | Pays Chinese merchants via Alipay | For paying businesses, not personal wallets ([Africa Brief](https://africabrief.substack.com/p/vodacom-tanzania-unveils-m-pesa-global)) |
| Kenya | SimbaPay via Family Bank PesaPap or M-Pesa | WeChat Pay | Recipient's phone number needed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1751255)); check it is still active |
| South Africa, Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Botswana, Malawi, Lesotho, UK | Mukuru | Alipay top-up, WeChat Pay | App or USSD ([Mukuru](https://www.mukuru.com/send-money/china/)) |
| Nigeria | Klasha | Chinese bank, Alipay, WeChat Pay | ([Klasha](https://klasha.com/blog/how-to-send-money-to-china-from-nigeria)) |
| Many countries | Taptap Send | Alipay, WeChat Pay, UnionPay card | Up to 50,000 CNY per transfer; Alipay recipient needs Chinese ID; WeChat first-timers must accept within 48 hours |
| Many countries | Wise, Western Union, WorldRemit, Remitly | Bank card; Alipay/WeChat for Chinese citizens | Best for sending to the student's own Chinese bank card |

**Best route for a foreign student**
- Open a Chinese bank account first (see Bank account)
- Send from home to that UnionPay bank card in your own name
- Link the card to Alipay and WeChat Pay; this also avoids the 3% foreign-card fee
- Before the bank account exists, rely on the foreign card linked to Alipay
**Pay with your home wallet:** some foreign e-wallets work directly at Chinese merchants through Alipay+, including AlipayHK, Kakao Pay, Naver Pay, Toss Pay, Touch 'n Go, TrueMoney, Changi Pay, OCBC Digital, HiPay, MPay and NayaPay ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240407756281/en)).
**Agent tips**
- Warn against informal cash agents who promise to load an Alipay balance; money can be lost with no protection.
- Tanzania has foreign exchange controls on money sent abroad, so large transfers may need documents ([Afriex](https://www.afriex.com/post/how-to-send-money-from-tanzania-to-china)).
- Fees and supported countries change often; the agent should show the user how to compare the final CNY amount across two apps before sending.

## SIM card
Every SIM in China needs real-name registration, and foreigners register with their original passport at an official carrier store ([Chongqing government](https://english.cq.gov.cn/services/liveinchongqing/MobileandInternetServices/202606/t20260609_15738878.html)). Students staying long-term need a +86 number for banks, school systems and many apps.
**Steps**
1. Go to an official store of China Mobile (中国移动), China Unicom (中国联通) or China Telecom (中国电信). Find one by searching the Chinese name in a map app ([Shenzhen foreign affairs](https://www.lg.gov.cn/english/services/usefulInfo/content/post_11229336.html)).
2. Bring the original passport (students should also bring the visa or residence permit). Stores may also do a live face scan.
3. Choose a monthly plan, pay, and receive the SIM. Airport counters take about 15–30 minutes.
4. Insert the SIM; if data doesn't work, check the carrier's APN setting (for China Mobile: cmnet).
**Key facts** ([bento.travel, checked July 2026](https://www.bento.travel/china/sim-card))

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| ID required | Original passport; no exceptions |
| Lines per passport | About 3 across all carriers |
| Tourist plan price | About 100–300 CNY for 15–30 days; e.g. China Unicom 30 days, 20 GB, about 200 CNY |
| Cheapest place | Downtown carrier stores; airport kiosks cost more |
| Top-up | WeChat Pay, Alipay or the carrier's app |

**Which carrier**
- China Unicom: most foreigner-friendly, with English help at international counters.
- China Mobile: largest network and best rural coverage, slightly more expensive.
- China Telecom: sometimes cheaper packages.
**Agent tips**
- Short-term tourists can use a travel eSIM instead and skip registration; students staying months should get a real +86 SIM.
- Avoid SIMs from convenience stores or street sellers; they may not be properly registered.
- When leaving China for good, cancel the number at a carrier service center.
- The Voice Room can speak for the user at the counter: "I want a monthly plan with about 20 GB of data."

## Bank account
International students should open a Chinese bank account soon after arriving; it unlocks transfers, red packets, scholarships and full Alipay/WeChat Pay features. A valid passport is always needed, and some banks also ask for the residence permit ([CCNU international students office](https://is.ccnu.edu.cn/info/1025/1216.htm)).
**Recommended banks:** Bank of China (中国银行, most international, best chance of English staff), ICBC (中国工商银行), China Construction Bank (中国建设银行) and Agricultural Bank of China (中国农业银行). These have the most branches across China. Some universities have an on-campus branch or help students open accounts together, so check with the international office first.
**Documents to bring**
- Original passport (name must match exactly on every form)
- Valid student visa (X1/X2) or residence permit
- Student ID card or admission letter
- Accommodation registration form (住宿登记表) or dorm contract, if asked
- Chinese phone number (+86) in your own name, for SMS codes
- 10–15 CNY for the opening deposit
**Steps at the bank** (from CCNU guide)
1. Go on a weekday; most banks close on weekends. Say: 我想开户 (I want to open an account).
2. Hand documents to the receptionist, who prints what is needed.
3. Fill in the application form; a sample is usually on the desk.
4. Take a queue number and wait to be called.
5. Give the forms to the clerk, pay the small deposit, and set a 6-digit PIN.
6. Receive a UnionPay debit card. Ask staff to help install the bank's app and turn on mobile banking.
7. Link the new card to Alipay and WeChat Pay.
**Agent tips**
- Some branches send foreigners to a specific branch that handles foreign clients; the agent should suggest calling ahead or asking the university.
- If the residence permit is still being processed, bring the receipt the Entry-Exit Bureau gives in place of the passport.
- The Voice Room is most useful here: bank counters rarely speak English, and the agent can explain the account type and answer the clerk's questions.

## Residence registration and residence permit
Every foreigner must register their accommodation with the police within 24 hours of arriving, and X1 student-visa holders must get a residence permit within 30 days of entry ([Zhejiang University](https://ism.zju.edu.cn/2025/1219/c89547a3121335/page.htm)). Missing either can make a student's stay illegal, so the agent should treat these as top-priority reminders.
**1. Accommodation registration (within 24 hours)**
- Living on campus: the international dorm reception usually registers the student at check-in and issues the form.
- Living off campus: go to the local police station (派出所) with the passport, visa and rental contract, together with the landlord, within 24 hours of moving in ([USTC](https://ic.ustc.edu.cn/en/v7info.php?Nav_x=21)).
- Hotels register guests automatically.
- Register again after moving, getting a new passport, or getting a new visa or residence permit.
- Keep the stamped form (住宿登记表): it is needed for the residence permit and often for the bank.
**2. Residence permit (X1 visa: within 30 days of entry)**
The X1 visa is single-entry and valid for only 30 days after arrival; the residence permit replaces it and allows multiple entries ([USTC](https://ic.ustc.edu.cn/en/v7info.php?Nav_x=21)). X2 (short-term) students stay on their visa and do not get a residence permit.
1. Get the accommodation registration form.
2. Do the official health check at the city's International Travel Health Care Center, or have a foreign medical report verified there. Book online in advance.
3. Get an introduction letter from the university's international students office.
4. Apply at the city Exit-Entry Administration Bureau (出入境管理局) on a working day.
5. Collect the permit after about 7–10 working days. The bureau keeps the passport and gives a receipt to use meanwhile.
**Typical documents for the permit** (example: [Tongji University, Shanghai](https://study.tongji.edu.cn/en/info/1030/1059.htm))
- Passport
- Admission notice (original and copy)
- JW201 or JW202 form (original and copy)
- Health check report from the city health center
- University introduction letter
- Accommodation registration form (original and copy)
- Passport photo
Tongji lists the permit fee as 400 CNY for up to 1 year and 800 CNY for 1–2 years, plus about 500 CNY for the health check; amounts vary by city and should be checked locally.
**3. Extensions and changes**
- Apply for an extension at least 30 days before the permit expires ([Zhejiang University](https://ism.zju.edu.cn/2025/1219/c89547a3121335/page.htm)).
- After a change of name, passport or school, update the permit within 10 days.
**Agent tips**
- Tell new students not to arrive too early before check-in, since the 30-day clock starts at entry.
- Each university has its own office hours and order of steps; the agent should send students to their international students office first.
- Use the Voice Room at the police station and health center, where English is rare.

## Cash, exchange and common problems
Mobile payment covers almost everything, but newcomers should still carry about 100–300 CNY in cash for small stalls, donation boxes or a dead phone ([Tianwen](https://tianwenwangluo.com/en/wechat-pay-for-foreigners/)). The central bank's policy is card payments for large amounts, QR payments for small ones, and cash always accepted as a last resort ([China Daily](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202403/02/WS65e26803a31082fc043ba22f.html)).
**Getting cash:** exchange at a bank branch or airport counter with the passport, or withdraw from ATMs of major banks that accept foreign cards. Avoid street money changers.
**Common problems and fixes**

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Card declined when linking | Home bank blocks Chinese wallets or online foreign use | Call the bank to allow it; check name matches passport; avoid many retries (may trigger a hold) |
| Passport verification stuck | Blurry photo or typing mistake | Redo the upload in good light, matching passport exactly |
| SMS code not arriving | Home SIM roaming off | Turn on roaming, or set up before leaving home |
| Payment refused at a shop | Merchant type doesn't accept foreign cards | Try the other wallet (Alipay ↔ WeChat Pay) or pay cash |
| Can't send money to a friend | Transfers and red packets need a Chinese bank card | Open a Chinese bank account |
| WeChat Wallet missing | Pay feature turned off | Me → Settings → General → Manage Services → WeChat Pay |
| Big payment costs extra | 3% fee above 200 CNY | Use a UnionPay card (no fee on Alipay) or a Chinese bank card |

Fixes for linking and verification come from [Tianwen's tested Alipay guide](https://tianwenwangluo.com/en/alipay-for-foreigners/).
**Agent tip:** when a payment fails, the agent should first ask which wallet and card the user tried, then suggest the matching fix from this table.

## App guides: how to use the most popular apps
Most daily apps can be opened as mini-programs inside WeChat or Alipay, so newcomers can start there instead of installing many apps ([China Guidelines, July 2026](https://chinaguidelines.com/en/posts/online-shopping-in-china)).
**DiDi (滴滴出行): taxis and ride-hailing**
DiDi has over 70% of China's ride-hailing market in more than 400 cities, and allows registration with a foreign number, an English interface and international cards ([China Guidelines](https://chinaguidelines.com/en/posts/didi-taxi)).
1. Use the DiDi mini-program in Alipay (no extra setup) or download the DiDi app and register with your phone number and SMS code.
2. In the app: Account → Wallet → Payment Methods; add Visa, Mastercard, JCB, Diners Club, Alipay or WeChat Pay. Password-free auto-pay is capped at 500 CNY per ride.
3. Enter the destination (copy the address from Amap if unsure), choose Express, Comfort or Premier, and confirm.
4. Check the plate number; the driver asks for the last 4 digits of your phone number, so keep a screenshot of them.
5. Chat with the driver in-app; messages are translated automatically.
6. Safety: share trip, emergency contacts, and a one-tap alarm linked to police (110). At airports, go to the signed ride-hailing pickup zone.

**Steps for any shopping order** ([China Guidelines](https://chinaguidelines.com/en/posts/online-shopping-in-china))
1. Have Alipay or WeChat Pay working, a Chinese delivery address (building, unit, room) and a Chinese phone number for the courier.
2. Check the item title, size or model, price, seller and recent reviews with photos.
3. Enter the address and check the delivery estimate at checkout.
4. Pay, then track the order; parcels may go to a locker or pickup point.
5. Returns: many items allow a 7-day no-reason return; start it from the order page and keep packaging and chats.
6. On Xianyu, keep chat and payment inside the app; never pay a stranger directly.
Foreign cards linked to Alipay work for physical goods on Taobao and Tmall, but not for Xianyu, pre-sale items or Tmall International ([Tianwen](https://tianwenwangluo.com/en/taobao-in-english/)).

## Emergencies and safety
China has no single emergency number: call 110 for police, 120 for an ambulance, 119 for fire and 122 for traffic accidents ([China Navigators, March 2026](https://www.chinavigators.com/china-emergency-numbers/)). Operators may not speak English, so the agent should be ready to say the user's location and problem in Mandarin.

| Need | Number | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Police (警察) | 110 | Crime, theft, disputes |
| Ambulance (急救) | 120 | Medical emergencies; Beijing also has 999 |
| Fire (消防) | 119 | Fires and rescue |
| Traffic accident (交通事故) | 122 | Also call 120 if anyone is hurt |
| Tourist help and complaints | 12301 | Scam reports; English lines in Beijing (010-12301) and Shanghai (021-12301) |
| Tourist police (旅游警察) | At major sites | Faster than 110 for small scam disputes |

**Embassies**
- Tanzania: Embassy in Beijing, No. 8 Liangmahe Nanlu, Sanlitun, Chaoyang District; phone +86 10 6532 1491 or 6532 1408; email beijing@nje.go.tz ([embassy site](https://www.cn.tzembassy.go.tz/about/staff/category/former-ambassadors)). Visits are by appointment.
- Other countries: the [Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://www.mfa.gov.cn) lists all embassies; the agent should save the user's embassy number at onboarding.
- Embassies can issue emergency travel documents, contact family and list English-speaking doctors and lawyers, but cannot pay bills or override Chinese law.
**Lost or stolen passport**
1. Report to the local police station (派出所) to get a loss report (遗失证明).
2. Contact your embassy for an emergency travel document or new passport.
3. Take the new passport and the police report to the city Exit-Entry Administration to get a new visa or residence permit; this can take up to five working days.
4. Students: tell the university international office and register accommodation again.
Keep a photo of the passport data page in email or cloud storage; it speeds up replacement.

## Healthcare
Foreigners should go to a hospital's international department (国际部), which has English-speaking staff and faster service at a higher price; patients pay before treatment or discharge ([China Navigators, March 2026](https://www.chinavigators.com/medical-help-foreigners-china/)). For emergencies, call 120.
**Steps when someone needs a doctor**
1. Emergency: call 120 or take DiDi to the nearest hospital emergency room (急诊).
2. Students: check the campus clinic and the student insurance first; universities usually require international students to hold Chinese medical insurance, so ask the international office which hospitals it covers.
3. Tourists: call the travel insurer's 24/7 line; some clinics bill insurers directly.
4. At the hospital, register (挂号) with your passport at the international department desk, or at the general registration desk or hospital WeChat account in smaller cities.
5. Pay the deposit (Alipay, WeChat Pay, cards or cash), see the doctor, pay for tests and medicine at the payment window.
6. Keep every receipt, the diagnosis and test results for insurance claims.

## Transport
In cities, newcomers mostly use the metro (scan a QR code in Alipay or WeChat), DiDi and shared bikes; between cities, high-speed rail is usually the fastest option up to about 1,200 km ([China Guidelines, June 2026](https://chinaguidelines.com/en/posts/high-speed-train)).
**Metro and buses:** open Alipay or WeChat, search for the city's metro or bus card (e.g. "metro" or 乘车码), activate it, and scan the QR code at the gate. Amap shows lines, stations and exits.
**DiDi and taxis:** see App guides. At airports and big stations, follow signs to the ride-hailing pickup zone; avoid people offering rides inside the terminal.
**Shared bikes:** Meituan, HelloBike and DiDi bikes unlock by scanning the bike's QR code in Alipay or WeChat; park in marked areas.
**High-speed trains**

| Booking option | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Trip.com | First trips, short stays | English, international cards, small service fee; can reserve up to about 60 days ahead |
| Railway 12306 | Long-term residents | No fee; needs passport and selfie verification (3–5 working days); tickets open 15 days before |
| Station window | Changes, refunds, help | Bring original passport; may not accept foreign cards |

1. Book with the passport name and number; confirm the right station (many cities have several, e.g. Beijing South vs Beijing West).
2. Train numbers: G (up to 350 km/h), D (250 km/h), C (200 km/h). Second class is fine for most trips.
3. Arrive 30–60 minutes early with the original passport; use the manual channel, since automatic gates may not read passports.
4. Go through security, find the gate number on the screens, then platform, carriage and seat number.
5. Changes and refunds: free change more than 48 hours before; refunds are free 8+ days before, then 5–20% depending on timing.
6. Holidays (Spring Festival, Labor Day, National Day) sell out fast; book as soon as sales open.

## Scams and normal prices
Most scams follow the same script: a friendly stranger, an invitation, and a bill nobody agreed to ([China Navigators, March 2026](https://www.chinavigators.com/common-scams-in-china/)). The agent's scam alerts should compare any quoted price with the normal prices below.
**Common scams**

| Scam | How it works | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Tea ceremony | Friendly "students" near a tourist site invite you to tea; bill is 500–2,000 CNY per person | Never accept invitations from strangers who approach you |
| Art students | "We are art students, come to our exhibition"; cheap art sold for thousands | Decline and walk away |
| Fake taxi (黑车) | Unlicensed drivers at stations and airports demand more at the end | Use DiDi or the official taxi queue with a meter |
| Fake monk | Bracelet or card pressed into your hand, then a "donation" demand of 100–500 CNY | Don't accept items; real monks don't solicit on the street |
| Overpriced restaurant | No prices on the menu, huge bill | Ask for a menu with prices first; check Dianping |
| Jade or gem "factory sale" | Fake or overpriced stones | Only buy from fixed-price shops |
| "It's closed today" | Driver diverts you to a shop for commission | Check opening times on Amap |
| Street money exchange | Fake notes or short counts | Exchange only at banks or official counters |

**Student-specific risks:** fake landlords asking for deposits before a viewing, "agents" offering to load Alipay balances for cash, and job offers that would break visa rules (see Rules for students).
**If it is happening now:** stay calm, photograph the bill and menu, and for larger amounts call 110 or find tourist police (旅游警察); for small amounts paying and leaving may be safest.
**Normal prices (guide only; varies by city)**

| Item | Typical price |
|---|---|
| University canteen meal | About 6–25 CNY ([WentChina](https://wentchina.com/cost-of-living-in-china-international-students/)) |
| Local restaurant meal | 30–60 CNY; Western restaurant 100–300 CNY ([Ikky in China](https://www.ikkyinchina.com/2025/01/10/monthly-budgeting-expats-china/)) |
| Metro ride | 3–9 CNY |
| Student monthly budget, Suzhou example | About 3,700 CNY (dorm, canteen, bus) to 9,600 CNY (studio, Western food, taxis) ([XJTLU](https://www.xjtlu.edu.cn/en/campus-life/suzhou-life/living-costs)) |
| Rent deposit | Usually one month's rent |

**Agent tip:** when a quoted price is more than about 2–3× the normal range, the agent should whisper a warning to the user and suggest asking for a written price first.

## Housing: renting off campus
Students living off campus must register the new address at the local police station with the landlord within 24 hours of moving in ([USTC](https://ic.ustc.edu.cn/en/v7info.php?Nav_x=21)), so the agent should treat "Will the landlord help with police registration?" as the first question for any flat. Many universities also require students to ask permission before moving off campus.
**Steps**
1. Ask the international office whether off-campus living is allowed and what record it needs (e.g. Tongji's "Record of Living off Campus").
2. Search listings through a registered agency, the university's notice boards, or apps such as Beike (贝壳) and Lianjia (链家); view the flat in person before paying anything.
3. Check the landlord's ownership certificate (房产证) and ID, and make sure the person signing is the owner or has written authority.
4. Agree the payment pattern; a common one is 押一付三 (one month's deposit, then three months' rent at a time). Deposits are usually one month's rent ([XJTLU](https://www.xjtlu.edu.cn/en/campus-life/suzhou-life/living-costs)).
5. Sign a written contract and keep a copy (see checklist).
6. Within 24 hours, go to the police station with the landlord, passport and contract to register.
7. Photograph the flat's condition on move-in day so the deposit can be returned.
**Contract checklist**
- Rent, payment dates and deposit amount
- Who pays water, electricity, gas, internet and property fees (物业费)
- Agency fee, if any (often up to one month's rent)
- Notice period and penalty for leaving early
- Repairs: who pays for what
- Landlord agrees to help with police registration
- Inventory of furniture and appliances
**Agent tips**
- Never transfer a deposit before viewing the flat and seeing the ownership certificate; fake landlords are a common scam.
- Utilities are usually paid in Alipay or WeChat (search 生活缴费, "living payments").
- The Voice Room is useful for viewings and contract questions with Chinese-speaking landlords.

## Rules students must know
The rules that most often get international students into trouble are working without permission, letting documents expire, and drugs. The agent should raise these early and plainly, without lecturing.
**Work and internships** ([Chongqing government, June 2026](https://english.cq.gov.cn/services/studyinchongqing/202606/t20260609_15739572.html))
- Off-campus work or internships are only allowed with university approval and a note added to the residence permit by the Exit-Entry Administration; without that note, off-campus work is not allowed.
- Eligibility: at least 18, a study residence permit with more than six months left, good conduct, and usually more than one year at the current school.
- Hours are capped at 8 per week and 40 per month during term, and 16 per week and 80 per month in vacations.
- Work only within the city of the university; a change of employer must be reported within 10 days.
- Online freelancing, tutoring or delivery jobs for Chinese clients count as work too; the agent should tell users to check with the international office first.
**Documents**
- Keep the residence permit valid; apply to extend at least 30 days before expiry (see Residence section).
- Overstaying is an offence under the Exit-Entry Administration Law and can lead to fines, detention or deportation.
- Carry the passport (or the Exit-Entry receipt while the permit is processed); police may ask to see it.
- Register the address again after every move or new permit.
**Drugs:** China treats drug use very seriously; police can test people, and foreigners caught using or carrying drugs face detention and deportation. The agent should never help find drugs.
**Other rules**
- Do not take part in unregistered religious preaching or large unapproved gatherings on campus.
- Do not sell or lend your bank card, SIM card or payment account to anyone; they are often used for fraud and the owner is held responsible.
- Leaving and re-entering: a valid study residence permit allows multiple entries; X2 visa holders should check their entries before travelling.
**Agent tip:** for anything legal, the agent gives general information and sends the user to the university international office or the Exit-Entry Administration for a definite answer.

## Culture, etiquette and holidays
A few habits make daily life smoother, and the holiday calendar matters because offices and banks close and trains sell out during the long breaks ([CLI, updated October 2026](https://studycli.org/chinese-holidays/2027/)).
**Etiquette basics**
- Greetings: a nod or handshake; say 你好 (nǐ hǎo). Use titles with surnames for teachers, e.g. 王老师 (Teacher Wang).
- Meals: dishes are shared; don't stick chopsticks upright in rice; the host often pays, and offering to pay is polite.
- Gifts: give and receive with both hands; avoid clocks and sets of four. Fruit or snacks from home are good gifts for teachers and hosts.
- Tipping: not expected.
- Bargaining: normal in markets, not in malls, supermarkets or apps.
- Queues and crowds: expect pushing at busy stations; stay patient.
- Face (面子): avoid criticising someone in public; raise problems privately and politely.
- WeChat is used for everything, including teachers and landlords; reply politely and avoid voice messages to people you don't know well.

## Weather and climate by region
China's climate ranges from cold, dry winters in the north to warm weather all year in the far south; the agent uses this to match places to a user's home climate and to warn about seasons. ## Visa-free entry by nationality
Whether a visitor needs a visa depends on their passport; the lists below come from [China Briefing (updated March 2026)](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/china-visa-free-travel-policies-complete-guide/) plus the August 2026 transit additions. Visa-free entry is never allowed for study, work, residence or journalism, so students always need an X visa ([CNTO](https://www.cnto.org.uk/last-news-en/china-expands-240-hour-transit-and-hainan-30-day-visa-free-entry-policies/)). Most Tanzanian, Kenyan, Nigerian and other African passports are not on the visa-free lists, so these travellers need a visa, except Mauritius and Seychelles.
**1. Mutual visa exemption (ordinary passports)** — usually up to 30 days per stay and 90 days in any 180 days for tourism, business and family visits:
Albania, Antigua and Barbuda, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Bahamas, Barbados, Belarus, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Dominica, Ecuador, Fiji, Georgia, Grenada, Kazakhstan, Malaysia, Maldives, Mauritius, Qatar, Samoa, San Marino, Serbia, Seychelles, Singapore, Solomon Islands, Suriname, Thailand, Tonga, United Arab Emirates, Uzbekistan.
**2. Unilateral 30-day visa-free (trial, runs to 31 December 2026)** — tourism, business, family visits, exchanges and transit:
- Europe: Andorra, Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Malta, Monaco, Montenegro, Netherlands, North Macedonia, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Russia, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, United Kingdom
- Asia and Middle East: Brunei, Japan, South Korea, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Kuwait, Bahrain
- Americas: Argentina, Brazil, Canada, Chile, Peru, Uruguay
- Oceania: Australia, New Zealand
**3. 240-hour (10-day) visa-free transit** — must hold an onward ticket to a third country, enter through one of 65 designated ports, and stay in permitted areas. Covers 57 countries: the Schengen states, the UK, Ireland, Russia, Ukraine and other European states, the US, Canada, Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Brunei, Indonesia, the UAE, Qatar, plus Kyrgyzstan and Vietnam from 20 August 2026.
**4. 24-hour transit** — any nationality transiting with a connecting ticket may stay up to 24 hours, within the arrival city.
**5. Hainan 30-day visa-free** — 61 countries, only within Hainan province; not for study or long-term work.
**6. Other routes**
- Residence permit holders (work or study) can leave and re-enter many times while the permit is valid.
- APEC Business Travel Card holders (most APEC economies, not the US or Canada) can enter for business up to 60 days at a time.
- Some ASEAN tour groups can enter Guilin or Xishuangbanna visa-free for up to 6 days; cruise tour groups can enter at 13 coastal ports for up to 15 days.
**People excluded from visa-free transit:** passports valid under three months, a refusal stamp from a Chinese visa office, illegal entry, stay or work in China in the last five years, or serious registration violations in the last two years.
**Agent tip:** these lists change several times a year; the agent should check the user's nationality against the National Immigration Administration (nia.gov.cn) or a Chinese embassy before giving a yes or no.

## Laws and rules foreigners must follow
Foreigners are fully subject to Chinese law, and some rules differ sharply from other countries; the summary below follows the [UK government's China travel advice (updated October 2026)](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/china/safety-and-security). The agent should explain these neutrally and send users to their embassy or a lawyer for real legal problems.

| Topic | Rule | What the agent should say |
|---|---|---|
| ID | Always carry your passport; police do random checks and don't accept photocopies | Keep the passport (or Exit-Entry receipt) with you |
| Drugs | Zero tolerance, including cannabis; random tests, bar raids with hair and urine tests; a positive test can be prosecuted regardless of where the drug was taken | Never use or carry drugs, even if legal at home |
| Fights | Fighting can bring fines or detention whoever started it; injured parties often claim compensation | Walk away and call 110 |
| Protests and politics | Avoid demonstrations and large gatherings; posting or supporting political statements critical of the authorities may be treated as illegal | Stay out of protests |
| National security | Laws are broad; activity outside China, including online, can count | Be careful with photos of sensitive sites and contacts with officials |
| Military areas | Restricted zones; photos near military or official sites can lead to detention or deportation | Don't photograph soldiers, bases or police |
| Religion | Five recognised religions; worship at registered venues; foreigners' group activities limited to foreigners; no preaching to Chinese citizens or distributing materials without approval; rules updated 1 May 2025 | Join registered mosques, churches or temples |
| Drones | Import, possession and use strictly regulated; registration with civil aviation authority | Don't fly drones without checking rules |
| Internet | Google, Facebook, YouTube and X are blocked; VPN services must be government-licensed | Use Chinese apps; follow the law |
| Driving | A Chinese driving licence and insurance are required; drink-driving at very low levels is punished harshly | Use DiDi or public transport |
| Money | Counterfeit 100-yuan notes exist, even from ATMs; banks won't replace them | Check notes; prefer mobile payment |
| Business disputes | Exit bans can stop people leaving China during disputes | Get legal advice before contracts |
| Detention | Police can hold someone up to 15 days of administrative detention without trial | Ask to contact your embassy; don't sign documents you can't read |

**Other everyday rules**
- Register your address within 24 hours of arrival or moving (see Residence section).
- Don't overstay your visa or permit.
- Don't sell, lend or rent out your SIM card, bank card or payment account; they are used for fraud and the owner is responsible.
- Same-sex relationships are legal, but public attitudes can be conservative.
- Check fire exits and gas safety in rented flats; carbon monoxide from gas heaters has caused deaths.
- Typhoons and floods are common from May to November in the south and east.
**Agent tip:** the agent states rules plainly and calmly; it never helps users get around laws, and for arrests, exit bans or court matters it sends them straight to their embassy.

## Staying in touch with home
WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, Google and X are blocked on Chinese networks, so newcomers need a plan before they arrive ([UK government](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/china/safety-and-security)). Hao recommends three lawful routes: a travel eSIM or home SIM on roaming, which send mobile data through servers outside mainland China, and WeChat for family at home. Hao does not recommend unlicensed VPN apps, because Chinese law requires VPN services to be government-licensed.
**Options compared**

| Option | How it works | WhatsApp / Instagram | Phone and OS needed | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Travel eSIM (international routing) | Roams on Chinese networks but routes data abroad | Usually work on mobile data | eSIM-capable, unlocked phone (see below) | Tourists, first weeks for students |
| Home SIM on roaming | Your home operator's roaming routes data home | Usually work on mobile data | Any phone; roaming switched on | Short stays; keeping home number for SMS codes |
| Chinese SIM (+86) | Local network | Blocked | Any unlocked phone | Daily life, banks, apps (see SIM card) |
| Dual SIM: Chinese SIM + travel eSIM | Chinese SIM for calls and local apps, eSIM for data abroad | Work when eSIM data is on | Dual-SIM phone with eSIM | Students staying long term |
| WeChat with family | Family installs WeChat at home | Not needed | iOS or Android, at home and in China | Free calls and video with family |
| Campus or home Wi-Fi | Chinese network | Blocked | Any | Chinese apps only |

Important: the roaming routes only help on mobile data. On campus or home Wi-Fi the phone uses the Chinese network, so blocked apps stop working.
**Which phones support eSIM (iOS and Android)**

| OS | Phones that support eSIM | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| iOS (Apple) | iPhone XS, XR and every model since, including iPhone SE (2020, 2022) | iPhones bought in mainland China, and most from Hong Kong and Macao, have no eSIM |
| Android – Samsung | Galaxy S20 and later, recent Z Fold, Z Flip and Note models | Some US and Korean versions lack eSIM |
| Android – Google | Pixel 3 and later | Some early Pixel 3 models excepted |
| Android – others | Many recent Xiaomi, Oppo, OnePlus, Motorola, Honor and Huawei models | Check the model |

To check any phone, dial *#06#: if an EID number appears, it supports eSIM. The phone must also be unlocked to all networks ([Monzo help](https://monzo.com/help/monzo-mobile/esim-compatibility)).
**Travel eSIM providers and their routing**
In tests published by eSIM seller Yesim, these providers' China plans let WhatsApp and Instagram work: Airalo, Airhub, Breeze, eSIM4Travel, Eskimo, Firsty, Holafly, Instabridge, Jetpac, Maya Mobile, Truely, Ubigi, Voye and Yesim (routed via Singapore, Poland, the UK or South Korea). Nomad, Saily and Three HK route through Hong Kong and may still block some apps such as TikTok or AI tools ([Yesim, 2026](https://blog.yesim.tech/?p=41202)). Routing can change, so users should check the plan before buying.
**Setup steps**
1. Before flying, check the phone supports eSIM (*#06#) and is unlocked.
2. Buy a plan with confirmed mainland China coverage, not a general Asia plan.
3. Install the eSIM by QR code while still at home, and test WhatsApp on it.
4. Install WeChat and ask family to install it too; add each other before you travel.
5. On arrival, turn on data roaming for the eSIM line and use it for mobile data.
6. Students: add a Chinese SIM as the second line for banks, deliveries and Chinese apps.
**Agent tips**
- Travel eSIMs are priced for short trips; for months-long stays, compare the cost with a home-operator roaming bundle and remind users that WeChat calls with family are free.
- Keep the home SIM active if banks or WhatsApp at home send SMS codes to it.
- If a user asks for VPN apps, the agent explains the licensing rule and offers these options instead.
